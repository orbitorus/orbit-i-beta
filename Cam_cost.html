How it works

We compute the classical Hohmann two-burn transfer between two circular orbits (one-way). ΔV per event = ΔV_up + ΔV_down (round-trip double). Propellant uses Tsiolkovsky rocket equation:
m_p = m0 * (1 - exp(-Δv/(Isp*g0)))

You can optionally enable mass bookkeeping to subtract consumed propellant after each maneuver and recompute the subsequent maneuvers with the new mass (precise if many maneuvers).

Thruster suggestions

Common picks (rough):

  • Isp 300 s — small chemical monoprop (typical thrust: ~0.03 N for cubesat RCS)
  • Isp 1500 s — Hall/EP (typical nominal thrust: ~0.1–1 N depending on device)
  • Isp 2000–2500 s — advanced EP/ion (low thrust: 0.05–0.5 N)

These are suggestions. For accurate timing & pointing, consult thruster datasheets.

Notes & tips

• For very small Δh (meters), ΔV is tiny (fractions of m/s) and propellant is grams — the linear approximation m≈m0 Δv/(Isp g0) is close.
• Low-thrust continuous spirals (electric propulsion) change time & losses but not the ideal ΔV significantly in this simplified model.
• To include drag, attitude-control margins, or station-keeping, add margin factors to ΔV or cost.
