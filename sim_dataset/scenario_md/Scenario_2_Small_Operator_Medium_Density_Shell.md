# Scenario 2: Small Operator in Medium-Density Shell

## Baseline Parameters
- Constellation size: 50 satellites
- Altitude: 620 km
- High-risk events: 5 per year
- Maneuver cost: $2,000 per event
- Total asset value: $50M
- Insurance premium rate: 2% annually

---

## Strategy 1: Pure Avoidance (Maneuver Only)

### Annual Maneuver Cost
5 events/year × $2,000 = $10,000

### Expected Loss Avoided
5 × 0.9 × $1,180M × 1e-4 = $531,000

### Net Benefit
$531,000 − $10,000 = $521,000 annually

Limitation: Maneuvers cannot protect against untrackable debris or moderate-risk events.

---

## Strategy 2: Pure Insurance

### Insurance Premium
$50M × 2% = $1,000,000 annually

### Coverage Limitations
- Coverage capped at asset value ($50M)
- Environmental and third-party liability ($1.13B) remains uninsured
- Premium escalation after claims likely

---

## Strategy 3: Combined Strategy (Optimal)

### Annual Costs
- Maneuvers (high-risk events): $10,000
- Insurance premium: $1,000,000  
**Total:** ~$1.01M annually

---

## Why This Is Optimal

- **Pure Avoidance**: Cheap but incomplete risk coverage
- **Pure Insurance**: Transfers asset risk but does not reduce collision probability
- **Combined Strategy**: Reduces collision likelihood and caps financial exposure

## Key Insight
Small operators cannot self-insure systemic collision risk.  
The optimal strategy combines **selective maneuvering** with **insurance coverage** to manage both probabilistic and financial risk efficiently.
