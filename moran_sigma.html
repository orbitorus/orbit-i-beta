Interpretation Guide

Moran's I Values

Moran's I > 0.3

Positive spatial autocorrelation. Similar values cluster together (e.g., disease hotspots, income segregation)

Moran's I < -0.3

Negative spatial autocorrelation. Checkerboard pattern where neighbors are dissimilar (rare in nature)

Moran's I ≈ 0

Random spatial pattern. Location doesn't predict value (sigma-based methods may be sufficient)

Statistical Significance (z-score and p-value)

Z-score

Measures: How many standard deviations the observed Moran's I is from expected value under randomness.
|z| > 1.96 → Significant at 95% confidence
|z| > 2.58 → Significant at 99% confidence
|z| > 3.29 → Significant at 99.9% confidence

P-value

Measures: Probability that the spatial pattern occurred by random chance.
p < 0.05 (*) → Statistically significant
p < 0.01 (**) → Very significant
p < 0.001 (***) → Highly significant
Lower p-value = stronger evidence of spatial pattern

Sigma-based Statistics

Mean (μ)

The average value of all data points. Represents the "center" of your distribution. Half the values are above it, half below.

Standard Deviation (σ)

Measures how spread out values are from the mean. Larger σ = more variability. Smaller σ = values closer to mean.

±1σ (68% of data)

Most sensitive threshold. Captures ~32% of data as outliers. Values within 1 standard deviation are "typical". Use when you want to catch moderately unusual values.

±2σ (95% of data)

Standard threshold. Captures ~5% of data as outliers. Commonly used in statistics and quality control. Good balance between sensitivity and specificity.

±3σ (99.7% of data)

Very conservative threshold. Captures ~0.3% of data as outliers. Only flags the most extreme values. Use when false alarms are costly.
