Why space-based LOS differs from ground:
A space sensor in a nearby orbit can view the target from any RTN angle — including pure in-track (±T) or cross-track (N) geometries that are geometrically impossible from the ground. This fills in the RTN axes that ground sensors leave unobserved.

Key insight: Set Space LOS azimuth = ±90° (in-track) to see the fused σT collapse — the axis ground sensors struggle with most.
◈ HOW INFORMATION FUSION WORKS IN RTN
① Ground Radar
Tight along ground-LOS (range), wide perpendicular. Typically strong σR constraint when LOS ≈ Radial. Weak in σT and σN.
② Ground Optical
Tight in angle (T, N), huge along ground-LOS. Complements radar — constrains the axes radar misses, but adds large σR uncertainty.
③ Space Optical
Closer range → tighter σtrans. No atmosphere. Unique geometry (e.g. pure in-track LOS) constrains σT directly — impossible from ground.
④ Fused Result
P-1fused = Σ P-1i — information adds. Volume shrinks as ∝ 1/√(det Pfused). Shape becomes compact when sensors cover all 3 RTN axes.
