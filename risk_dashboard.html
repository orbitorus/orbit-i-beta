04 — TRACKING INFRASTRUCTURE

Why space-based tracking changes everything

Ground-based radar is limited by land availability and object size (>10 cm). Orbitorus is built on a space-based sensing constellation tracking debris down to 1–3 cm.
Ground-based tracking (status quo)
Detection floor: ~10 cm
Radar placement constrained by land access and political geography
Poor southern hemisphere and polar coverage
1–2 updates per object per day → slow covariance refinement
~1 million objects in the 1–10 cm range are completely invisible
1–10 cm is the "lethal but untrackable" zone — too large to stop with shielding, too small to see
Covariance realism poor — operators apply arbitrary 2–10× inflation factors
Space-based tracking — Orbitorus constellation
Detection floor: 1–3 cm
Sensors in orbit have shorter range to targets → dramatically better sensitivity
Global coverage including poles and southern hemisphere
Multiple observations per orbit → covariance collapses hours earlier
Sub-10 cm debris enters the catalogue for the first time
Higher cadence enables Bayesian sequential updating — each pass refines the posterior
Earlier covariance convergence = cheaper maneuvers = directly lower mission risk and insurance cost
The Orbitorus value chain: Space-based constellation (sensor layer) → Bayesian sequential estimation with ensemble drag models (intelligence layer) → Continuously updated Pc with calibrated uncertainty bands (risk layer) → Quantified maneuver trade-space for operators and underwriters (decision layer). Each layer is impossible without the one below it.
