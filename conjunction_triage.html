How scoring works

Truth first. Each scenario starts from a true miss distance. Noisy CDMs are generated from it about every 12 hours, from about 72 h to 12 h before closest approach. As in real data, the timing is irregular and an update is sometimes missing. About 30% of collision events are genuinely close, far more than in reality (in ESA's public CDM data, 0.8% of events end high-risk), so poor judgment shows up within one session. Some events carry optimistic covariance: the reported uncertainty is about a third of the real spread. A Pc below 10⁻¹² is shown as ≈0, as real CDMs often report zero.

Patterns from real data. Four event shapes found in ESA's public CDMs appear in the sets. Sharp collapse: Pc reaches a notable level, then one poor orbit update reports it as near zero (Set A). Resolves itself: early CDMs look close, later ones show the objects pass far apart, and closing is right (Set A). Thin evidence: the first CDM arrives only 36 h out and the second shows a drop (Set B). Dilution: Pc stays low only because the uncertainty is huge (Set B). Each random session has one. Closing an event on a sudden collapse, or on a couple of CDMs while the risk is not negligible, is scored as a poor decision even if nothing happens.

Costs. A maneuver costs fuel and mission downtime, rising from 25 at 72 h to 55 at 12 h. A tracking pass costs 8 and makes later CDMs sharper and honest about their uncertainty. A handoff to SDA review costs 5 in analyst time. A maneuver is required when the realistic Pc reaches the maneuver threshold, the operator's risk policy, set in the header (1×10⁻⁴ by default). It is the same for every event and every update: costs change how to act, never how much risk is accepted.

SDA handoff events. With the switch on, one event per session involves an active satellite that changes its orbit between updates. Its collision probability stays low, so collision logic alone says dismiss. What matters is the pattern: a deliberate approach closes in step after step, each step larger than the uncertainty band can explain. A routine station-keeping burn shifts the geometry once and then stops. Handing off with at least two consistent approach steps is a sound call. Handing off routine behavior costs analyst time and weakens the credibility of future flags. Judging intent stays with the SDA analysts; this desk only decides whether to pass it on.

Two scores. Outcome is what actually happened. Decision quality compares your call with what a calibrated analyst would conclude from the same information. That separates a good call from a lucky one.

Sample only. Geometry is simplified to a 2D encounter plane with isotropic uncertainty and a 20 m combined hard-body radius. Approach patterns are generic and do not describe any real operator or capability.
