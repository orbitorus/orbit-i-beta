Step 1 — Belief weight and evidence update
The foundation. Assign a weight to each hypothesis about where an object might be. When you observe something, weights shift — evidence for one takes weight from the other.
Key insight
A missed detection is not a non-event. When the sensor is reliable (P=0.90) and sees nothing, that is strong evidence the object is not in A. Silence is loud. Weight is conserved — whatever leaves A goes to B exactly.

Step 1+ — Unknown mass m(?)
Standard Bayes forces all belief into named hypotheses. m(?) reserves mass for objects not yet catalogued — making the model open-world.
Key insight
When evidence arrives that neither A nor B explains well, it flows into m(?) rather than awkwardly inflating the wrong hypothesis. A growing m(?) signals catalogue incompleteness — a dark object may be present.

Step 2 — Physical pruning (hard elimination)
Some hypotheses are not merely improbable — they are physically impossible. Apply constraints to permanently remove them from the set.
Key insight
Physical elimination produces weight = exactly 0, not a very small number. No future observation can raise it. This is deductive certainty — the only true certainty in your model. Always prune before evidence updating.

Step 3 — Time propagation and σ growth
Without new observations, position uncertainty σ grows continuously. Belief weight m(h) stays stable — but operational usefulness m_eff decays.
Key insight
A 7-day-old TLE and a fresh TLE with the same nominal orbit have identical m(h) — but radically different m_eff. Treating them as equivalent produces dangerously optimistic conjunction probabilities. m(h) is stable; m_eff is time-sensitive.

Step 4 — Negative evidence (missed detection)
When the sensor looks and sees nothing, that is real evidence. A miss penalises tight hypotheses more than diffuse ones.
Key insight
Detection → updates m(h) AND resets σ tight (positional info gained). Miss → updates m(h) only, σ unchanged (no positional info). Repeated misses erode tight hypotheses and grow m(?). At 0.20 the spawn threshold fires.

How to use this panel
  • Set days low (1 day) — hypotheses are tight. Fire several misses. Watch h1 lose weight rapidly.
  • Set days high (6 days) — hypotheses are diffuse. Fire misses. Weight shifts much more slowly.
  • Fire 7–12 consecutive misses at low days. Watch m(?) climb to the spawn threshold.
Step 5 — Unknown mass lifecycle
m(?) grows from three distinct sources and, when large enough, spawns a new named hypothesis. This is the discovery mechanism.
Key insight
C is born with wide σ (8 km) and moderate weight. It immediately enters the full update cycle — prunable by physics, tightenable by detection, retirable by misses. The remaining 40% of m(?) stays unknown — there may be more objects beyond C.
