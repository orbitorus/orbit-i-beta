The Dynamic Model between Knowledge and Belief

A visual guide to how beliefs form, resist, and change

Every belief sits in a valley. The heavier the belief, the deeper the valley.

A new idea sits in a shallow valley — easy to push out. A long-held belief sits deep — it has survived many challenges and grown very heavy.

Evidence pours in from the top like water — trying to reach the belief at the bottom.

The same wave reaches the shallow belief easily, barely grazes the medium one, and can't touch the deep one at all.

When a well grows too deep — no ordinary wave can reach the bottom.

Confirmation bias isn't a character flaw. It's what happens when a belief has grown so deep that ordinary evidence can't reach it. To move it, you'd need something extraordinary.

Knowledge is subtraction — what's left after the impossible is ruled out.
